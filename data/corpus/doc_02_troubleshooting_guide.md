# Industrial Rotary Compressor Troubleshooting and Diagnostic Guide

## §1 Acoustic Fault Analysis and Knocking Sounds
Mechanical knocking sounds are critical diagnostic indicators in Model 7 rotary screw compressors. Knocking originates from three primary mechanical root causes:
1. **Worn Bearings**: Degraded radial or axial thrust bearings cause rotor shaft play and mechanical knocking under load.
2. **Loose Mounting Bolts**: Slack foundation anchor bolts or loosened motor-to-airend flange bolts produce severe resonant rattle.
3. **Low Lubricant Oil Level**: Insufficient fluid volume in the 12L oil reservoir breaks down hydrodynamic cushioning between rotor lobes, creating boundary chatter.

### Knocking Severity Classification
- **Knocking at Startup**: If a knocking sound occurs only during startup transients and subsides within 30 to 60 seconds as oil pressure normalizes, it is classified as **Low Concern**. Log the event and inspect bearing clearances during next scheduled maintenance.
- **Continuous Knocking Under Load**: Rhythmic or continuous knocking while operating under loaded conditions (at 125 PSI) represents a **High Concern** fault. Equipment **must be stopped within 24 hours** to prevent catastrophic air-end seizure.

### Immediate Diagnostic Steps for Knocking
Upon detecting any knocking sound, technicians must immediately execute these steps:
1. **Check Oil Level**: Inspect the optical sight glass while running loaded. Fluid level must be between 50% and 75%. Top off with Synthetic PAO ISO 46 if below 50%.
2. **Inspect Bearings**: Use an acoustic stethoscope or vibration pen on bearing housings to identify localized spalling or cage damage.
3. **Tighten Mounting Bolts**: Check and tighten all 4 foundation bolts and flange fasteners to the specified 110 Nm torque.

## §2 Mechanical Vibration Anomalies
Baseline vibration should remain below 4.5 mm/s RMS. Vibration between 4.5 mm/s and 7.1 mm/s RMS indicates coupling wear or minor misalignment. Any vibration exceeding 7.1 mm/s RMS requires an immediate trip shutdown to protect rotor shafts from permanent deflection.

## §3 Thermal Overheating and Temperature Excursions
Standard operating discharge temperature ranges from 75°C to 90°C. Discharge temperature exceeding 100°C indicates thermal distress:
- Inspect aluminum cooler fins for lint and dust accumulation; clean using reverse compressed air.
- Verify the thermostatic bypass valve opens fully at 71°C for oil flow to the radiator.
- Automated thermal trip shuts the machine down at 105°C.

## §4 System Pressure Drops and Flow Reductions
When system discharge pressure falls below 125 PSI under nominal demand:
- Inspect intake air filter differential; service cartridge if restriction exceeds 0.8 bar.
- Test minimum pressure check valve (MPV) poppet spring to verify it maintains 65 PSI internal pressure.
- Check blowdown solenoid valve for pneumatic leakage venting to the atmosphere during loaded cycles.

## §5 Lubricant Leaks and Fluid Containment
Inspect airend shaft seals, scavenge lines, and cooler couplings for fluid seepage. Retorque flared O-ring fittings to 35 Nm. If shaft seal leakage exceeds 5 drops per minute, isolate the compressor and replace the mechanical shaft seal assembly.
