# Operational Safety Protocols and Fault Risk Classification Standards

## §1 Equipment Fault Risk Classification Matrix
To safeguard personnel and equipment during industrial air compressor operation, all operational conditions for Model 7 and Model 9 compressors are categorized into three standardized risk classifications:
- **Green Status (Normal Operation)**: All physical telemetry parameters (discharge pressure 125 PSI, discharge temperature 75°C–90°C, vibration < 4.5 mm/s RMS, noise <= 78 dB) reside within baseline tolerances. Standard unmonitored production is permitted.
- **Yellow Status (Caution / Minor Fault)**: Minor operational anomalies detected that pose no immediate catastrophic danger. Examples: transient knocking at startup that subsides within 60 seconds, minor oil weeping (< 5 drops/min), or maintenance overdue by < 48 hours. Continued operation is permitted under restricted conditional monitoring.
- **Red Status (Critical Hazard / Severe Fault)**: Acute mechanical failure or severe hazard active. Examples: continuous knocking under load, metal-on-metal screeching, discharge temperature > 100°C, smoke, burning odor, or pressure > 140 PSI. Standard production is prohibited; machine must be stopped under emergency procedures.

## §2 Yellow-Status Maximum Operation Hours and Monitoring Limits
When an asset enters Yellow Status, the following operational limits apply:
- **Maximum 72-Hour Continuous Operation Limit**: Equipment running with an active Yellow-Status fault is restricted to a **maximum allowed continuous operation limit of 72 hours**. No asset may operate past 72 consecutive hours with an unresolved Yellow fault.
- **Bi-Hourly Parameter Logging**: Operators must manually record temperature, line pressure, and acoustic observations every 2 hours in the shift log.
- **Automatic Red Status Escalation**: If the 72-hour window expires without resolution or scheduled parts replacement, status automatically escalates to Red Status, mandating immediate power-down.

## §3 Red-Status Protocols and Mandatory Buddy System
When a compressor is flagged under Red Status:
- Equipment must be stopped either immediately (for thermal or pressure spikes) or within a maximum of 24 hours (for continuous knocking under load) to prevent rotor seizure.
- **Mandatory Buddy System**: Whenever technicians or engineers work within 3 meters of an operating or hot Red-Status compressor, a **mandatory two-person buddy system** is required.
  - One technician performs inspection or diagnostic readings.
  - The designated safety buddy remains stationed outside the 3-meter zone with clear line of sight, standing directly beside the Emergency Stop button and electrical isolator.
  - Solo work on Red-Status equipment is strictly prohibited.

## §4 Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) Requirements
Personnel operating within the 2-meter compressor enclosure perimeter must wear certified PPE:
- **Hearing Protection**: Due to the Model 7's 78 dB(A) rated noise output, earplugs or earmuffs with a minimum Noise Reduction Rating (NRR) of 26 dB are mandatory.
- **Eye Protection**: ANSI Z87.1 approved safety glasses with side shields.
- **Hand Protection**: Heat-resistant nitrile-coated mechanics gloves rated for continuous contact up to 100°C.
- **Safety Footwear**: ASTM F2413 compliant steel-toed boots with oil-resistant non-slip soles.

## §5 Lockout-Tagout (LOTO) Procedures
Before opening panels or servicing the Model 7 compressor:
1. Depress the mechanical Emergency Stop button on the cabinet face.
2. Disengage the 400V 3-phase electrical circuit breaker at the distribution panel; apply a red padlock and lockout tag.
3. Vent trapped pneumatic pressure from receiver and separator vessels until gauges read 0.0 PSI.
4. Verify zero energy state by attempting a restart before beginning mechanical disassembly.
